## MICROSOFT INNOVATION CLUB, VIT CHENNAI Deepfake-Resistant Executive Transaction Authorization

Cybersecurity

- 1. BACKGROUND

Finance teams, executive assistants, help desks, banks, and payment operations increasingly receive urgent instructions through phone calls, video meetings, and messaging platforms. A request may appear to come from a CFO asking for an emergency transfer, a senior executive requesting a credential reset, or a trusted vendor announcing a last- minute change in beneficiary details. Generative voice and video systems can now imitate familiar people, while caller-ID spoofing and compromised collaboration accounts make the communication appear technically legitimate.

Most enterprise controls authenticate a device, account, or communication channel. They do not prove that the real executive originated the request or that the person understood and approved the exact transaction being executed. This creates a gap between communication identity and transaction intent — precisely where

deepfake vishing and business-email-compromise workflows operate.

## Core Issues

- Human trust exists outside the transaction control — Employees are conditioned to respond quickly to senior leadership, especially when a request is confidential or urgent. A convincing voice or video can therefore override normal caution.

- Deepfake detection alone is not sufficient — Detection models may fail against new generators, noisy telephone audio, edited recordings, short utterances, or a real voice replayed in a misleading context.


- Authentication does not establish intent — Even a genuine executive account can be compromised, and a valid voice does not prove approval of a specific payee, amount, account, or deadline.

- High-pressure requests bypass normal workflows — Attackers deliberately use secrecy, authority, urgency, and channel switching to persuade staff to skip verification or weaken approval thresholds.

- Existing evidence is fragmented — Telephony metadata, meeting- session risk, device identity, payment details, employee behavior, and approval records are usually reviewed in separate systems after an incident.

The real problem is not simply identifying synthetic audio or video — it is preventing any high-risk financial or privileged action from being

authorized solely because a communication looks or sounds genuine.

## Target Beneficiaries

Corporate finance and treasury teams, banks, payment processors, executive offices, IT help desks, and security operations teams. Secondary beneficiaries: auditors, insurers, vendors, and customers affected by

impersonation-driven fraud.

## Impact

- Prevents high-risk transfers, credential resets, and beneficiary changes from being approved through voice or video trust alone

- Reduces exposure to deepfake vishing, executive impersonation, replay attacks, and compromised communication accounts

- Preserves usable business workflows through risk-based verification instead of blocking every executive request

- Measures effectiveness through attack-block rate, legitimate approval success, false-challenge rate, verification time, and prevented fraudulent value
